# 📖 Market research team: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your research team. Start with Setup: three answers, then two things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is a research agency. A team signs clients, frames each client's question, sizes the market from public sources, tears down competitors and counts what buyers say. Every figure carries its source. A CRM holds every lead and client.

Every email waits for your yes. Every report is filed for your review before a client sees it.

**You:** say which markets you cover and which decisions your research supports. Approve emails and review each report. Present findings to the client.

**The team:** plans each client's week every Monday, and writes market sizing, competitor teardowns and voice-of-customer reports.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer three questions. Your team reads them before each job.

- **What does your agency do, and for whom?** "Market research for founders raising a seed round."
- **What do you sell, and for about how much?** Your packages, as you quote them.
- **Which markets do you cover, and what decisions do clients make with your research?** "B2B software in Europe. Whether to enter a market, and how to price."

On day one:

- [ ] Connect your email domain.
- [ ] Add each client you already have, with the decision they face, to the CRM.

> ⚠️ A figure with no source doesn't go in a report. If the team can't source a number, it says so. Never fill a gap with a guess dressed as a fact.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

- **New business is your agency's.** Its sales finds and signs clients for every team, and hands each one to the research lead.
- **Research lead.** It runs each client and drafts each update. It asks before any email goes out.
- **Desk researcher** frames each client's question as one decision and sizes the market from public sources. It shows the method: top-down, bottom-up, or both, with each input sourced.
- **Competitor analyst** tears down each competitor in one table: pricing, positioning, product, funding and stack, each cell sourced.
- **Voice-of-customer analyst** reads what buyers say about the client and its competitors in public reviews and forums, and counts the themes. It quotes, and names where each quote came from.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

- **Every Monday:** the week ahead. The research lead plans each client's week.

Research runs when a card asks for it. For a new project:

1. You write the decision on a card: "Should Acme enter Germany next year?"
2. The desk researcher frames the question and sizes the market.
3. The competitor analyst builds the table.
4. The voice-of-customer analyst counts the themes.
5. The research lead compiles one report. It waits in Needs you for your yes, and the CEO tells you in your chat.
6. You review it and write the recommendation.

The team does only the pieces the card asks for. Ask for a sizing alone and no teardown runs.

Each task has a cap. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

**For every project**

- [ ] Write the decision in one sentence before any research starts.
- [ ] Ask what the client already believes, so the report tests it.
- [ ] Agree the deliverable and the date with the client.
- [ ] Check every figure has a source you can open.
- [ ] Write the recommendation yourself. The team supplies the evidence.

**Every day**

- [ ] Approve or edit emails.
- [ ] Review filed reports.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read each client's week ahead.
- [ ] Check each lead has a next step and a date.

> 💡 Lead every report with the answer, then the evidence. Clients read the first page.

## 💸 Monetization

- **Fixed-price reports.** Market sizing, a competitor teardown or a buyer study, each at its own price.
- **Bundles.** All three for one decision, at one price.
- **Monthly watch.** A short monthly update on competitors and buyer themes.
- **A briefing call.** An hour walking the client's team through the findings, sold with any report.

Agree up front what the client may share. Investors and boards often see your report, so say whether your name stays on it.

Price on the decision's value to the client and never below the team's spend. The CEO can read that spend. Put your packages in your setup answer. This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns.

## 🌱 Growth

1. **Own one market.** Sources and tables repeat, and each project gets faster.
2. **Turn reports into a watch.** Offer every finished project a monthly update.
3. **Publish one free finding a month,** with nothing private in it. It shows your work.
4. **Add a market** once the first one is steady.
5. **Keep a source list.** Save the public sources that held up in a doc in Playbooks. Every seat can read Playbooks. Tell the analysts to start from it, so the next project begins with trusted sources.

Don't grow by taking projects with no decision attached. "Tell us about the market" never ends. Ask for the decision, or decline.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **The market size swings between methods.** Show both and explain the gap. Ask the desk researcher which input moves the result most.
- **A competitor's data is old.** Mark its date in the table. Ask the analyst to look for a newer public source.
- **Reviews are too few to count.** Say the sample is small. Widen the sources or the period, and report quotes rather than counts.
- **A client wants a number the sources don't support.** Say so plainly. Offer a range with its method instead.
- **Two sources disagree.** Show both, with their dates and methods. Say which you trust more and why.
