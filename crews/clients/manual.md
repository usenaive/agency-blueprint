# 📖 Client team: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your agency's client team. Start with Setup: two answers, then two things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This team runs your agency's client work. Its lead runs each account, and Delivery does the client work. Your agency's sales finds and signs the clients. A CRM holds every lead and client. A calendar holds every call and follow-up. The Ads app holds every ad for review, and the SEO & GEO app holds the search work.

You bring the trade and the judgement. Every email to a lead or a client waits for your yes. Every piece of client work is filed for your review before it goes out.

**You:** say what you sell, to whom, and for about how much. Approve emails and client work. Take the calls the team books.

**The team:** plans each client's week every Monday, and drafts the client work and files it for review.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer two questions. Every agent reads them before each run.

- **What does your agency do, and for whom?** One sentence, with the buyer in it: "We build simple websites for local dental clinics."
- **What do you sell, and for about how much?** Your main offer and its rough price: "A five-page site at a fixed price, live in three weeks." Use your real price. The team quotes only what you write here.

On day one:

- [ ] Connect your email domain, so the team writes from your address.
- [ ] Add any client you already have to the CRM, with one line on what you do for them.

> ⚠️ Write a price you would really charge. A lead who books a call expects the number they read.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

Each seat does one job. They hand work on through the board, and the CEO keeps them pointed at your plan.

- **New business is your agency's.** Its sales finds and signs clients for every team, and hands each one to the client lead.
- **Client lead** runs each client: the kickoff, the calls, the weekly update and the renewal. It drafts each update and asks before it sends.
- **Delivery** does the client work: research, drafts, pages and reports. Each piece is filed in Files for your review. Ask it "show me what is waiting for review".

To change a seat's job, tell the CEO in plain words. To change what a seat may do alone, open the agent and change its tools. Sending email always asks.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

The team works on timers. Between them, nothing runs and nothing spends.

- **When you add the team:** the client lead meets your clients. It books a kickoff for each client already in the CRM, or asks you once which clients you already have.
- **Every Monday:** the week ahead. The client lead plans each client's week and drafts the updates.

Only when you ask:

- **Client work.** Give Delivery a card: "Draft the homepage copy for Smile Dental."
- **A proposal or a case study.** Ask your agency's sales or content writer.

Change a timer when clients want more contact (add a Thursday check-in for the client lead).

Each timer has a cap per run. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

**Every day**

- [ ] Open the approval queue. Read each email as the person who gets it.
- [ ] Approve, edit or hold. Your edits teach the team your voice.
- [ ] Review any client work Delivery filed.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read the week ahead for each client.
- [ ] Check the CRM: every lead has a next step and a date.
- [ ] Pick the one client who needs you most this week.

**After every call**

- [ ] Add three lines to the client or lead in the CRM: what they want, what worries them, what happens next.
- [ ] Ask the client lead to draft the follow-up.

> 💡 Keep the CRM true. Every seat reads it. A lead with no next step is a lead nobody works.

## 💸 Monetization

An agency earns from fees. Pick the shape that fits the work.

- **Project fee.** One price for one result: a site, an audit, a campaign. Good for a first job with a new client.
- **Monthly retainer.** A fixed monthly price for ongoing work. Good once a client trusts you.
- **Tiers.** Three versions of one offer, small to full. Most buyers pick the middle one, so make the middle one the one you want to sell.

To set your price:

1. Write down the hours a job takes you and the team's spend on it. The CEO can read the spend for you.
2. Decide what your time is worth.
3. Price on the result for the client, not only your hours, and never below your cost.

Put the price in your setup answer, so every proposal says the same number. This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns.

## 🌱 Growth

Grow in this order.

1. **Win the first three clients.** Keep the offer narrow. One buyer, one result.
2. **Deliver well and write it down.** Turn each finished job into a case study, with the client's yes.
3. **Raise the price.** Once demand is steady, raise it for new clients.
4. **Add a retainer.** Offer every finished project a monthly follow-on.
5. **Add a specialism.** Get a specialist team from the Marketplace, such as SEO, ads or research, for the work clients ask for most.

Don't take every client. A client outside your lane costs more to serve and teaches the team the wrong thing.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **Leads don't reply, or are the wrong fit.** Leads are your agency's sales' work: tell it there.
- **A client says the update was late.** Check the Monday timer is on. Then ask the client lead to send updates on the day the client wants.
- **Client work misses the brief.** Write the brief as a card with three lines: what it is for, who reads it, what good looks like. Delivery works from the card.
- **An email went out with a mistake.** Write to the person yourself and fix it. Then tell the CEO the rule, so every draft follows it.
