# 📖 Recruiting team: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your recruiting team. Start with Setup: three answers, then three things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is a recruiting agency. A team signs clients who are hiring, turns each open role into a brief, finds people who already do that work, scores them the same way for everyone and drafts the first message to each. A CRM holds every client and lead. A calendar holds every call.

Nothing reaches a candidate without your yes. Every email to a client, a lead or a candidate waits for your approval.

**You:** say what you recruit for, where, and how you charge. Approve every message and every shortlist that goes to a client. Run the interviews and close the hire.

**The team:** plans each client's week every Monday, and writes role briefs, sources, screens and drafts outreach for each open role.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer three questions. Your team reads them before each job.

- **What does your agency do, and for whom?** "We hire engineers for seed-stage software startups."
- **What do you sell, and for about how much?** Your fee model in your own words. The team quotes only this.
- **Which locations and levels do you recruit for, and how do you charge?** "Remote in Europe, mid to senior, a percentage of first-year salary."

On day one:

- [ ] Connect your email domain, so the team writes from your address.
- [ ] Add each client you already have and their open roles to the CRM.
- [ ] Read the first role brief before any sourcing starts. Correct it.

> ⚠️ Judge people on the work the role needs, never on age, gender, race, religion, disability or any other protected trait. The screener scores only against the brief. Keep your own notes to the same rule.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

- **New business is your agency's.** Its sales finds and signs clients for every team, and hands each one to the recruiting lead.
- **Recruiting lead** runs each client: the kickoff, the weekly update on each role and the renewal. It drafts and asks before it sends.
- **Sourcer** turns each open role into a brief, then finds people who already do that work. It records where it found each person.
- **Screener** scores each shortlist against the brief, the same way for everyone, and writes the interview kit: the questions and what a strong answer sounds like.
- **Outreach writer** drafts a first message and two follow-ups for each person the screener put forward. Each one is personal to the person's work. Each waits for your yes.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

- **Every Monday:** the week ahead. The recruiting lead plans each client's week and drafts the role updates.

Role work runs when a card asks for it. For each open role:

1. You or the recruiting lead open a card: the role, the client and the deadline.
2. The sourcer writes the brief. You approve it.
3. The sourcer finds people and files the list.
4. The screener scores them and files the shortlist and the interview kit.
5. The outreach writer drafts messages for the people you approve.

The recruiting lead compiles the finished brief, shortlist, kit and sequence into one file for the client. It waits in Needs you, and the CEO tells you in your chat. Nothing reaches the client until you approve it. The team does only the steps the card asks for.

Each timer and each task has a cap. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

**For every new role**

- [ ] Kickoff with the client: must-haves, nice-to-haves, salary range, location and the interview steps.
- [ ] Approve the brief before sourcing.
- [ ] Review the shortlist. Remove anyone you wouldn't put in front of the client, and say why.
- [ ] Approve each outreach message.

**Every day**

- [ ] Open the approval queue. Read each message as the candidate would.
- [ ] Reply to candidates who answered. Book the calls you want.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read each client's week ahead and each role's status.
- [ ] Close roles that are filled or paused. Tell the team.

> 💡 A short honest message wins more replies than a long pitch. Say why this person, why this role and what happens next.

## 💸 Monetization

Recruiting is paid by placement or by retainer.

- **Contingency fee.** Paid when a hire starts. Usually a share of first-year salary. Easy to sell, risky to deliver.
- **Retained search.** Part paid up front, the rest on hire. Better for hard roles.
- **Fixed project.** A set fee for a shortlist or a market map, when a client hires on their own.

Write a clear rule for a hire who leaves early, such as a free replacement within a set time. Put your terms in your setup answer, so every proposal says the same thing. This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns.

## 🌱 Growth

1. **Own one niche.** One kind of role in one market. Briefs and shortlists get faster each time.
2. **Keep a bench.** With their yes, keep strong candidates you didn't place in the CRM for the next role.
3. **Ask for the next role.** After each hire, the recruiting lead asks what the client hires next.
4. **Write case studies** with the client's yes.
5. **Add a market or a level** only once the first one runs steadily.

Ask every placed candidate, after their first months, how the process felt. Write what you learn into the brief template. Good candidates refer friends, and those referrals are often your best shortlist.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **Shortlists miss the mark.** The brief is vague. Add three must-haves and two deal-breakers to it, then ask the sourcer to search again.
- **Candidates don't reply.** The message is about the client, not the person. Ask the outreach writer to open with the person's own work.
- **A client keeps changing the role.** Write the new brief as a fresh card and ask the client to approve it. Start sourcing only after that.
- **A candidate asks to be removed.** Remove them from the CRM and tell the team never to contact them again. Reply to confirm.
- **The screener scored someone unfairly.** Read its notes against the brief. Fix the brief, then ask for a rescore. Scores must rest only on the work.
