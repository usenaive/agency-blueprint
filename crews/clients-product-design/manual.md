# 📖 Product design team: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run product design work with this team. Start with Setup: three answers, then two things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This team designs physical products for your clients. It turns a brief into files: a 3D concept of how a product looks, a part built to the millimetres the brief names, a studio picture of each look, and a short product video when the brief asks for one.

You add it to a company you already have, beside any other team. The CRM, the Calendar, the Ads app and the SEO & GEO app are shared with every other team in the company.

Every email waits for your yes. Every job reaches you once, in one review from the Product design lead, before a client sees it. Every video waits for your yes before it is made.

**You:** say what you design, for whom, and what must be buildable. Send briefs. Approve each job before a client sees it.

**The team:** plans each client's week every Monday, and makes looks, parts, pictures and videos.

> 💡 A brief with sizes in millimetres becomes a part. A brief without them becomes a look. Write briefs with that in mind.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer three questions. Your team reads them before each job.

- **What does your agency do, and for whom?** "Concepts and parts for independent homeware brands."
- **What do you sell, and for about how much?** Your packages, as you quote them.
- **What must be buildable to the millimetre, and what is a look only?** "Brackets and mounts to millimetre sizes; carafes and lamps are looks only." This decides which seat takes a brief.

On day one:

- [ ] Connect your email domain.
- [ ] Add each client you already have to the CRM, with what they make.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

Each seat does one job. Each chat works for one client.

- **New business is your agency's.** Its sales finds and signs clients for every team, and hands each one to the product design lead.
- **Product design lead.** It runs each client, sends each brief to the right seat, and drafts each update. It asks before any email goes out.
- **Product designer** turns a look brief into one textured 3D concept. A concept has no units, so it never claims a size or that the object can be made.
- **Engineer** builds a part to the millimetres a brief names and checks it is a closed solid of the right size. It saves a part only when every edge is within half a millimetre of the brief. Otherwise it stops and logs both sizes on the client.
- **Visualizer** renders one studio picture of a concept, from the concept file itself, never from the brief's words. It makes a product video only when the brief asks, and only after you approve it.

No seat makes an account under a made-up name or deceives a real person or company.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

- **Every Monday:** the week ahead. The product design lead plans each client's week.

Design work runs when a card asks for it:

1. The product design lead reads the brief and sends it to the product designer (a look) or the engineer (a part).
2. If the brief also asks for a picture or a video, the visualizer makes it from that file.
3. The product design lead writes one delivery note naming every file. It waits in Needs you for your yes, and the CEO tells you in your chat.

The team does only the pieces the card asks for. Each task has a cap. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

**Every day**

- [ ] Look at each file in the delivery note before you approve it.
- [ ] For a part, check the sizes in the note against the brief.
- [ ] Approve, or ask for changes with one line on why.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read each client's week ahead.
- [ ] Check each lead has a next step and a date.

> ⚠️ A concept look has no units. Never send one to a maker as if it were a part. Ask for a part with millimetres instead.

## 💸 Monetization

- **Concept packages.** A set of looks and studio pictures for a new product idea.
- **Dimensioned parts.** Files a maker can build from, priced by the part's complexity.
- **Product videos.** An add-on for a launch, once the look or part is approved.
- **Retainers.** A monthly fee for a brand that keeps sending briefs.

Price on the value to the client and never below the team's spend. Videos cost the most to make. This manual makes no claim about what a studio earns.

## 🌱 Growth

1. **Nail one product kind,** so every look is on brief.
2. **Build a portfolio.** With each client's permission, keep your best pictures to show the next one.
3. **Add parts.** Once looks sell, offer buildable parts to the same clients.
4. **Add another team.** For launch campaigns, add the advertising team from the Marketplace to this same company.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **A brief went to the wrong seat.** It named millimetres when it meant a look, or the other way round. Rewrite the brief, and say "look" or give sizes.
- **A part wasn't saved.** It wasn't within half a millimetre of the brief. Read the client's timeline, which shows both sizes, and correct the brief.
- **A picture looks nothing like the brief.** The picture shows the concept as it was made. Ask for changes to the concept.
- **A video was never made.** Videos need the brief to ask, and your yes.
