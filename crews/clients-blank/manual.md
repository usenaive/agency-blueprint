# 📖 Blank agency: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you build your agency's team, one seat at a time. Start with Setup: two answers, then four things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is a client services agency that starts with no team. You get a CRM that holds every lead and client, and a calendar that holds every call and follow-up. You tell your CEO what you sell, and it hires the agents you need.

Start here if you know your trade and want to choose who does what. If you'd rather start with a full team, the Client agency in the Marketplace comes with Sales, a client manager, Delivery and Marketing already hired.

**You:** say what you sell, to whom, and for about how much. Tell the CEO which jobs you want done. Approve what the team you hire sends and makes.

**The CEO:** reads your answers and proposes a team, hires each agent once you agree, and sets each agent's timer and cap with you.

> 💡 Hire one agent for the job that takes most of your week. Add the next once that one runs well.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer two questions. The CEO and every agent you hire read them before each run.

- **What does your agency do, and for whom?** One sentence, with the buyer in it: "We write grant applications for small arts charities."
- **What do you sell, and for about how much?** Your main offer and its rough price. Use your real price. The team quotes only what you write here.

On day one:

- [ ] Tell the CEO the first job you want done: "find leads", "follow up my calls", or "draft client reports".
- [ ] Read the team the CEO proposes. Say yes, or say what to change.
- [ ] Add the clients you already have to the CRM, with one line on what you do for each.
- [ ] Put your next client calls in the calendar, so the team plans around them.

> ⚠️ Nothing is hired until you say yes. An empty team spends nothing and does nothing.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

You start with no agents. Every seat you add is one you asked for. Three seats cover most agencies:

- **A sales seat** finds leads that fit your answers, writes a short first email, follows up and books calls. Ask the CEO to make every email wait for your yes.
- **A client seat** runs each client: the kickoff, the calls and the weekly update. It reads the CRM and the calendar. Ask it to draft updates and never send alone.
- **A delivery seat** does the work you sell, from your brief. Ask the CEO to have it file every piece for your review.

To hire, tell the CEO the job in one sentence, who it is for, and what it must always ask you first. The CEO writes the seat's brief, picks its tools and shows you before it hires.

To change a seat's job later, tell the CEO in plain words. To change what a seat may do alone, open the agent and change its tools.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

A blank agency has no timers. Nothing runs and nothing spends until you hire a seat and give it a time.

Good first timers:

- **A sales seat, each weekday morning.** It works the pipeline before you start your day.
- **A client seat, every Monday.** It plans each client's week and drafts the updates.

Only when you ask: a one-off job (give a seat a card: "Draft the report for Hill Farm."), or a proposal (ask the sales seat to write one from a call's notes).

You keep asking for the same thing? Ask the CEO to put it on a timer. A timer's work sits unread? Run it less often.

Each timer has a cap per run that you agree with the CEO. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

Your week, in three habits.

**Every day**

- [ ] Check the CRM for anything new. The CEO lists what waits on you.
- [ ] Approve or reject what your seats filed. Give one line on why.
- [ ] Log each call in the calendar, so the team knows what happened.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Look at the pipeline. Which leads moved, and which stalled?
- [ ] Ask the CEO whether a seat needs a new job or a new timer.

**Every month**

- [ ] Look at what each seat spent against what it made you.
- [ ] Remove a seat whose work you never approve.

> ⚠️ Don't hire a seat that may send email without asking. Keep sending on ask until you trust the seat's drafts.

## 💸 Monetization

An agency earns from clients who pay for work. The team helps you sell it. You set every price.

- **Retainers.** A monthly fee for ongoing work. This is the steadiest income.
- **Projects.** A fixed price for a fixed scope. Good for a first job with a new client.
- **Add-ons.** A second service for a client who already pays. Ask your client seat which clients might need more.

To price, start from the time a job takes you and what it is worth to the client. Write the price in your setup answer. The team quotes nothing else.

This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns. Keep your costs in view: every seat you hire has a cap, and the CEO can show you what each one spent.

## 🌱 Growth

Grow in this order. Move on only when the last step is steady.

1. **Win the first clients yourself.** Your own network is the fastest start. A sales seat can draft the notes for you to send.
2. **Hire for the job that eats your week.** Usually that is sales or delivery.
3. **Put repeat work on timers.** A weekly report or a Monday plan, so nothing waits on you to remember.
4. **Add a specialism.** If clients keep asking for one thing, get a specialist agency from the Marketplace.
5. **Write down how you work.** Edit this manual as you go. Every seat you hire can read it, and a buyer reads it if you ever sell the company.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **Nothing is happening.** A blank agency has no team and no timers. Tell the CEO the first job you want done.
- **The CEO proposed seats I don't need.** Say which job matters most. Hire that one seat first.
- **A seat's drafts miss the mark.** Its brief is too broad. Tell the CEO what good looks like, with one real example.
- **Leads are a poor fit.** Your first answer is too broad. Name the buyer, the size and the place.
- **A client says they got an email you didn't approve.** Open the seat and check its tools. Sending email should ask. Tell the CEO to set it back.
- **The CRM is out of date.** Ask your client seat to read the calendar and update each client once a week.
