# 📖 SEO & GEO agency: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your search agency with its team. Start with Setup: four answers, then four things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is an agency that wins search for its clients. It covers two kinds of search. SEO is ranking in the classic results. GEO is being cited when someone asks an AI engine the question.

A team of agents finds and signs clients, audits each client's site, writes the pages and articles that should rank, and tunes them so AI answers cite them. Your dashboard holds the CRM, the approval queue and your public site. Every email waits for your yes, and every piece of client work waits for your review.

**You:** say what you sell, to whom, and which competitors your clients lose to. Approve emails and review each audit, article and answer block. Take the calls the team books, and own each client relationship.

**The team:** works the pipeline each weekday morning, plans each client's week every Monday, audits sites worst issue first and turns the gaps into pages to write, and writes one page per search query, with answer blocks AI engines can quote.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer four questions. Every agent reads them before each run.

- **Your public name, a verified inbox and how you price.** The site uses these. Nothing is priced that you did not say.
- **What does your agency sell, SEO, GEO, content, and to whom?** Name the buyer: "Search for dental clinics in the UK."
- **Who is your ideal client?** Industry, size, place. Sales builds its first list from this.
- **Which three competitors do your clients lose to in search?** Real domains. The gap researcher compares your site against them.

On day one:

- [ ] Connect your email domain, so the team writes from your address.
- [ ] Approve the site builder's rewrite of your public site.
- [ ] Read the first leads Sales finds. Mark the good ones and say why the others miss.
- [ ] Add any client you already have to the CRM, with their domain and what you do for them.

> ⚠️ Never promise a ranking or a citation. No one controls either. Sell the work and the reporting, not a position.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

Seven agents run the agency, a paid-media planner proposes ads, and each client gets a team of four when its deal is marked won on the CRM.

- **Sales** finds leads that fit your answer. Before each first email it audits the lead's public site: what it ranks for and whether AI answers cite it. It writes the opener from one finding, keeps a dated next step on every lead and follows up. Tell it which sites to skip.
- **Client manager** runs each client: the kickoff, the weekly check-in and the referral ask.
- **Site builder** keeps your public site true to your answers. Every change waits for you.
- **Content writer** and **content reviser** write and sharpen your own blog, one search query a post.
- **Gap researcher** compares your site with the three competitors you named, every Friday.
- **Proposal writer** turns a qualified lead into a proposal, priced only from what you said.
- **Paid-media planner** proposes paid search and social plans. It never launches or spends.

Each client's team:

- **Audit runner** audits the client's site and rankings, worst issue first. Ask it "what are the three worst issues for this client?"
- **SEO writer** writes briefs, articles and landing pages, one search query each, from the audit and the client's own facts.
- **GEO optimizer** tunes pages to be cited by AI answers: short answer blocks, clear entities, schema and an llms.txt file.
- **Client lead** compiles each finished job for that client into one file and asks you once to approve it.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

The team works on timers. Between them, nothing runs and nothing spends.

- **Each weekday morning:** the pipeline pass. Sales reads the inbox, files replies and drafts follow-ups.
- **Every Monday:** the client check-in. The client manager reviews each client's calendar and drafts the update.
- **Tuesdays and Thursdays:** a new post for your own blog. **Wednesday:** the three weakest pages get a revision. **Friday:** the competitor gap report.

Client work runs when you ask a client's team for it. For a new client, usually:

1. The audit runner audits the site and files the report.
2. If you asked for pages too, it hands the missing pages to the SEO writer.
3. The SEO writer writes each page and files it for review.
4. If you asked for answer-engine work, the GEO optimizer tunes those pages.

The team does only what you asked. Ask for an audit alone and no pages are written.

Each member tells the client's lead what it filed. Once every piece you asked for is in, the client lead compiles them into one file and asks you once to approve it. A piece that comes later goes into the same file, with no second ask. Each draft still waits for its own approval in the queue, and nothing reaches the client until you approve it.

To audit every client each month, ask the CEO to add a monthly timer for the audit runner. Each timer has a cap per run.

## 📋 Operations

**Every new client.** Run this in order.

- [ ] **Kickoff call.** Ask: who approves work, who gets the weekly update, and what a great first quarter looks like in one sentence.
- [ ] **Access.** Get read access to the site's search console and analytics, and a way to publish pages. Note each one in the CRM.
- [ ] **Facts.** Collect the client's services, prices, places served and proof they can share. The writers use only these.
- [ ] **Competitors.** Confirm the three competitors for this client.
- [ ] **Baseline.** The audit runner records where the client stands today: pages, issues, rankings for ten target queries.
- [ ] **Plan.** Pick the first five pages from the audit. Agree them with the client.
- [ ] **Calendar.** Add the weekly update day and the monthly review to the calendar.

**Every day**

- [ ] Open the approval queue. Approve, edit or hold each email.
- [ ] Review filed pages and answer blocks against the client's facts.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read each client's week ahead.
- [ ] Check every lead in the CRM has a next step and a date.

> 💡 Review a page as the client's customer would read it. If a sentence sounds like nobody, cut it.

## 💸 Monetization

Search work suits monthly fees, because results build over months.

- **Audit as the first job.** A fixed-price audit with a page plan. It shows your work before a long commitment.
- **Monthly retainer.** A set number of pages and updates each month, plus the report.
- **Tiers.** Small: audit fixes and two pages a month. Middle: more pages plus GEO work. Full: everything plus a monthly call. Set the contents and prices yourself.

To set a price:

1. Ask the CEO what one page and one audit cost the team to make. It reads the spend.
2. Add your own review time per client.
3. Price on the value to the client, never below your cost, and write it in your setup answer.

This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns, or about the results a client will see.

## 🌱 Growth

1. **Prove it on three clients.** Keep the niche narrow so audits and pages repeat.
2. **Report clearly.** A monthly page of what changed, in plain words. Clients renew what they understand.
3. **Write case studies.** With the client's yes, Marketing turns a before and after into a post.
4. **Raise the price for new clients** once your calendar is full.
5. **Add paid search.** Clients who rank often ask for ads next. Get the advertising team from the Marketplace.

Keep a doc in Playbooks of the fixes that worked for past clients. Every seat can read Playbooks. Tell the audit runner to read it first, so each new audit starts from what you already know.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **The audit lists too much.** Ask the audit runner for the three issues that block the most pages. Fix those first.
- **Pages read the same.** The writer lacks the client's facts. Add their services, places and proof to the CRM, then ask for a rewrite.
- **A client asks why rankings dropped.** Ask the audit runner to compare this month with last. Explain what changed on the site, and what didn't.
- **AI answers still don't cite the client.** Check the page answers the question in its first lines. Ask the GEO optimizer to tighten the answer block and check the schema.
- **Leads don't reply.** Ask Sales to open with one specific thing it saw on the lead's site, and one question.
