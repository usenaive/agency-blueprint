# 📖 AI automation agency: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your agency with its team. Start with Setup: three answers, then four things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is an agency with no fixed specialism. It comes with a public site, a dashboard with a CRM, a calendar and a queue of work, and seven agents. They keep the site true to what you sell, work the pipeline, run each client, write the agency's own blog, fix weak pages, study competitors and draft proposals.

You set the offer and the price, and you decide what goes out. A site change waits for you. Every email waits for your yes. Every post, page and proposal is a draft until you approve it.

**You:** say what you sell, to whom, and how you price it. Approve site changes, emails and drafts. Take the calls and close the deals.

**The team:** rewrites your site from your answers on day one, finds and follows up leads each weekday morning, checks in on every client each Monday, and writes, revises and researches through the week.

> 💡 Start with one offer for one kind of client. You can widen it once the first clients pay.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer three questions, and a fourth if you like. Every agent reads them before each run.

- **What does your agency sell, and to whom?** One or two sentences. The site's hero is written from it.
- **Who is your ideal client?** Industry, size and place. Sales builds its first lead list from it.
- **How do you price?** Retainer, project or hourly, and your usual range. The site's pricing and every proposal use it. Leave it out and the team asks you before it quotes.
- **Who do you already know?** Optional. Name people who might need this, or know someone who does. Sales drafts a short personal note to each for you to send.

On day one:

- [ ] Read the site rewrite. Approve it, or say what to change.
- [ ] Read the first leads Sales files. Say why the weak ones miss.
- [ ] Approve the first email, or rewrite it in your voice.
- [ ] Replace the demo clients in the CRM with your own, or delete them.

> ⚠️ The CRM starts with demo rows marked as demo. Delete them before you show the dashboard to anyone.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

Each seat does one job and hands work on. The CEO keeps them pointed at your plan.

- **Site builder** keeps the public site in your words: hero, services, who you serve, process, pricing and FAQ. It proposes the whole rewrite as one change, and nothing goes live without you. It never writes a case study, a testimonial or a number you didn't give it.
- **Sales** finds leads that match your ideal client and files each with a reason to reach out now. It drafts first emails and follow-ups and moves leads through the pipeline. Every email asks first. Tell it who is not a fit.
- **Client manager** owns every active client: onboarding, the calendar and the weekly check-in. It flags a stall before the client notices. It drafts the check-in, and you send it.
- **Content writer, content reviser and gap researcher.** The researcher compares your site with named competitors and hands the gaps to the writer. The writer drafts posts for the agency's own blog. The reviser finds thin or stale pages and files a revision for each, at most three a run.
- **Proposal writer** works when Sales hands over a qualified lead. It writes scope, three package tiers from your pricing answer, and a timeline, as a note on the client. It never sends.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

The team works on timers. Between them, nothing runs and nothing spends.

- **Each weekday morning:** Sales reads the inbox, finds new leads and drafts follow-ups.
- **Monday morning:** the client manager checks every client and drafts the check-ins.
- **Tuesday and Thursday mornings:** the content writer drafts the next blog post from the calendar.
- **Wednesday:** the content reviser picks the weakest pages and files revisions.
- **Friday:** the gap researcher files the competitor gap report the writer works from.

Only when you ask, or when a teammate hands it on: a site rewrite after you change your offer, a proposal when Sales hands over a lead, or a one-off post on a topic you name.

Change a timer when there are too many leads to read (ask Sales for fewer and better ones), or when the blog runs ahead of you (drop one writing day).

Each timer has a cap per run. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

Your week, in three habits.

**Every day**

- [ ] Open the approval queue. Read each email as the person who gets it.
- [ ] Approve, rewrite or reject. Give one line on why. The reason teaches the team.
- [ ] Answer any question a card asks you.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read the client check-ins before they go.
- [ ] Look at the pipeline. Which leads moved, and which stalled?
- [ ] Tell the CEO one change for the week.

**Every month**

- [ ] Read the posts and pages that went live. Ask the reviser about the weakest.
- [ ] Check spend against each seat's cap. Raise a cap only for work you approve most.

> ⚠️ Don't approve an email that promises a result. The team never claims one, and you shouldn't let an edit add one.

## 💸 Monetization

An agency earns from clients who pay for work, again and again. The team helps you sell it. You set every price.

- **Retainers.** A monthly fee for ongoing work. This is the steadiest income. Sell it once a client has seen a first result.
- **Projects.** A fixed price for a fixed scope. Good for a first job with a new client.
- **Proposals in tiers.** The proposal writer gives three packages from your pricing answer, so a client picks a size instead of saying no.
- **Referrals.** Each Monday the client manager may propose one short email asking a happy client for an introduction. It offers no reward and claims no result.

To price, start from the hours a job takes you and the value it has for the client. Write the range in your pricing answer. The team quotes nothing else. This manual makes no claim about what an agency earns.

## 🌱 Growth

Grow in this order. Move on only when the last step is steady.

1. **Win the first three clients.** Use your own network first. Sales drafts the notes. You send them.
2. **Turn projects into retainers.** Ask the client manager which clients are ready for ongoing work.
3. **Publish proof.** Once a client agrees to be named, ask the writer for a case study from real results.
4. **Narrow the offer.** If one kind of client keeps buying, say so in your answers. The site and the lead list follow.
5. **Add a specialism.** Get a specialist agency from the Marketplace, such as SEO and GEO, when clients keep asking for it.

Never grow by taking work you can't deliver. A late client costs more than a lost lead.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **The leads are a poor fit.** Your ideal client answer is too broad. Narrow it: name the industry, the size and the place. Tell Sales who is not a fit.
- **The site still sounds generic.** The site builder writes from your answers. Make the offer answer specific, then ask for a new rewrite.
- **A proposal has no prices.** The pricing answer is empty. Add your range, or answer the proposal writer's question.
- **The blog writer filed nothing.** It needs the calendar the gap researcher writes on Friday. Ask the researcher to run now.
- **An email went out that shouldn't have.** Emails only send after your approval. Check who approved it, and add a line to the CEO about what never to send.
- **Nothing happened today.** Check the timers. If every card waits on you, the team waits too. Answer the oldest card first.
