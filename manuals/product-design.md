# 📖 AI product design agency: Operator's Manual

> 👋 This is how you run your design studio with its team. Start with Setup: four answers, then four things on day one.

## 🧭 Overview

This company is a design studio for physical products. Four agents turn a client's brief into files: a 3D concept of how a product looks, a part built to the millimetres the brief names, a studio picture of each look, and a short product video when the brief asks for one. A CRM holds every client, and a Studio screen holds every file waiting for you.

You decide what leaves the studio. Every file waits for your approval. No client gets an email until you approve the work, and every video waits for your yes before it is made.

**You:** say what you design, for whom, and what must be buildable. Send briefs, or let the studio lead read them from the inbox. Approve each file before a client sees it.

**The team:** writes the studio note from your answers on day one, routes each brief to the right seat, makes looks, parts, pictures and videos, and checks every Monday what is still waiting.

> 💡 A brief with sizes in millimetres becomes a part. A brief without them becomes a look. Write briefs with that in mind.

## 🛠️ Setup

You answer four questions. The studio lead writes them into the studio note, and every seat reads that note before it makes anything.

- **What physical products does this studio design?** Name the kinds: "Kitchen and desk objects: carafes, lamps, small brackets." The designer's first look is one of these.
- **Who is the work for?** The buyers: "Independent homeware brands." Pictures are styled for them.
- **What must be buildable, and what is a look only?** "Brackets to millimetre sizes; carafes and lamps are looks only." This decides which seat takes a brief.
- **What does done mean for a typical brief?** A concept, studio pictures, a dimensioned part, or a short video. A video is made only when this answer or the brief asks.

On day one:

- [ ] Read the studio note. Correct anything it got wrong.
- [ ] Approve or hold the first concept look and its picture.
- [ ] Add your real clients to the CRM, and delete the demo rows.
- [ ] Connect your email, so the studio lead can read briefs and draft replies.

> ⚠️ Until the studio note exists, no seat makes anything. If nothing appears on day one, check the note first.

## 🤝 Agent team configuration

Each seat does one job. The studio lead reads every brief and hands it on.

- **Studio lead** writes the studio note from your four answers, then reads each brief against it. A brief with millimetres goes to the engineer. Any other object brief goes to the designer. A video goes to the visualizer, once its look or part exists. It adds new clients to the CRM, and asks you when a brief names no way to reach them. It never emails a client before you approve the work.
- **Designer** turns a look brief into one textured 3D concept. A concept has no units, so it never claims a size or that the object can be made.
- **Engineer** builds a part to the millimetres a brief names and checks it is a closed solid of the right size. It files a part only when every edge is within half a millimetre of the brief. Otherwise it stops and notes both sizes on the client.
- **Visualizer** renders one studio picture of each concept, from the concept file itself, never from the brief's words. A part comes with its own preview from the engineer. It makes a product video only when the brief asks, and only after you approve it.

To change a seat's job, tell the CEO in plain words.

## 🗓️ Scheduling & automation

One timer runs. The rest of the work starts when a brief arrives.

- **Monday morning:** the studio lead lists every file still waiting for your approval. It notes on each client any brief that has gone more than three days with no file, and what it is waiting on. It makes nothing and sends nothing.

When a brief arrives:

- [ ] The studio lead reads it and routes it.
- [ ] The designer or the engineer makes the file.
- [ ] For a look, the visualizer renders its picture.
- [ ] Each file lands on the Studio screen for you.

Only when you approve it: a product video, and any email to a client.

Want a midweek check? Ask the CEO to add a Thursday check-in. Briefs are rare? Leave Monday as it is. The check-in costs little when nothing waits.

The timer has a cap per run. A run that would cost more stops instead.

## 📋 Operations

Your week, in three habits.

**Every day**

- [ ] Open the Studio screen. Look at each file before you approve it.
- [ ] For a part, check the sizes in the note against the brief.
- [ ] Approve, or hold with one line on why. The reason teaches the team.

**Every Monday**

- [ ] Read the studio lead's check-in notes.
- [ ] Unblock the oldest brief: answer its question, or add the missing size.

**Every month**

- [ ] Reread the studio note. Update it when your products or buyers change.
- [ ] Check spend against each seat's cap. Videos cost the most. Approve them on purpose.

> ⚠️ A concept look has no units. Never send one to a maker as if it were a part. Ask for a part with millimetres instead.

## 💸 Monetization

A design studio earns from clients who pay for files they can use. You set every price.

- **Concept packages.** A set of looks and studio pictures for a new product idea. Good for a first job.
- **Dimensioned parts.** Files a maker can build from, priced by the part's complexity.
- **Product videos.** An add-on for a launch, once the look or part is approved.
- **Retainers.** A monthly fee for a brand that keeps sending briefs.

To price, start from the time a brief takes you to review and correct, and what the files are worth to the client. Price videos above stills, since they cost more to make.

This manual makes no claim about what a studio earns.

## 🌱 Growth

Grow in this order. Move on only when the last step is steady.

1. **Nail one product kind.** Make the studio note narrow, so every look is on brief.
2. **Build a portfolio.** With each client's permission, keep your best pictures to show the next one.
3. **Add parts.** Once looks sell, offer buildable parts to the same clients.
4. **Add launch videos.** Offer a short video with every approved product.
5. **Add a second studio.** For a different product kind, get another from the Marketplace. Each one is its own company with its own team.

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

- **Nothing was made on day one.** The studio note is missing. Ask the studio lead to write it from your answers.
- **A brief went to the wrong seat.** It named millimetres when it meant a look, or the other way round. Rewrite the brief, and say "look" or give sizes.
- **A part wasn't filed.** It wasn't within half a millimetre of the brief. Read the note on the client, which shows both sizes, and correct the brief.
- **A picture looks nothing like the brief.** The picture shows the concept as it was made. Hold the concept, and say what to change in it.
- **A client got no reply.** Emails wait for your approval. Check the approval queue.
- **A video was never made.** Videos need the brief to ask, and your yes. Approve it from the card.
